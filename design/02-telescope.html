Penicillin was discovered in September 1928, when Alexander Fleming found a stray mould on a culture plate had lysed the surrounding staphylococci. He named the substance penicillin, but it took Florey and Chain at Oxford to purify it into a usable medicine, and the three shared the 1945 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
