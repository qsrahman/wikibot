Who discovered penicillin?

Penicillin was discovered in September 1928, when Alexander Fleming, then at St Mary’s Hospital in London, returned from holiday to find that a stray mould on one of his culture plates had destroyed the surrounding staphylococci. He isolated the mould — a Penicillium — and found its juice killed a range of bacteria, naming the substance penicillin.

Fleming alone could not turn it into a medicine. A decade later Howard Florey and Ernst Chain at Oxford purified and mass-produced it in time for the Second World War, and the three men shared the 1945 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
